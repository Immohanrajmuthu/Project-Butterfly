---

# Purpose

Every successful software project starts long before the first line of code is written.

It begins with understanding **why** the project exists.

Many projects fail not because the technology was wrong, but because the purpose was never clearly defined. Teams often jump directly into implementation—choosing frameworks, writing features, and solving technical problems—before agreeing on the problem they are actually trying to solve.

Project ButterFly encourages a different approach.

Before creating repositories, designing architecture, or selecting technologies, take time to understand the project's purpose.

A clear purpose becomes the foundation for every decision that follows.

# Why Purpose Matters

Imagine two teams building similar applications.

**Team A** begins coding immediately.

Within weeks they discover changing requirements, conflicting priorities, unnecessary features, and uncertainty about what truly matters.

**Team B** spends one hour defining their purpose before writing any code.

That single hour provides direction for months of development.

Purpose becomes a decision-making compass.

Whenever uncertainty appears, the team asks:

> **Does this help us achieve our purpose?**

If the answer is **No**, they confidently avoid unnecessary work.

Purpose reduces complexity before complexity ever appears.

# Activities

## Activity 1 — Discover the Purpose

### Objective

Understand why this project should exist.

### Questions

- Why are you building this?
- Why is now the right time?
- Why does this matter?

### Output

Write a one-sentence **Purpose Statement**.

**Example**

> KuttyCouture exists to make quality children's fashion easily accessible through a simple and trustworthy online shopping experience.

---

## Activity 2 — Define the Problem

### Objective

Describe the real problem—not the solution.

### Questions

- What challenge currently exists?
- Who experiences this problem?
- What happens if nobody solves it?

### Output

Write a concise **Problem Statement**.

---

## Activity 3 — Identify the Audience

### Objective

Understand who this project serves.

### Questions

- Who are the primary users?
- Who maintains the project?
- Who are the stakeholders?

### Output

Identify the project's audience and stakeholders.

---

## Activity 4 — Create a Vision

### Objective

Imagine the future.

### Questions

- Where should this project be in two years?
- What impact should it create?
- What will people say about it?

### Output

Write a concise **Vision Statement**.

---

## Activity 5 — Define Success

### Objective

Determine how success will be measured.

### Questions

- What outcomes indicate success?
- How will you know the project is delivering value?
- Which goals are measurable?

### Output

Create measurable **Success Criteria**.

---

## Activity 6 — Complete the Project Canvas

Bring everything together into a single document.

The Project Canvas becomes the foundation for every planning, design, and engineering decision throughout the project.

# Common Mistakes

### Starting with technology

Do not choose a framework before understanding the problem.

---

### Defining features instead of purpose

Purpose explains **why**.

Features explain **what**.

They are not the same.

---

### Trying to solve every problem

Clearly define what your project will and will not address.

Focus creates quality.

---

### Ignoring the audience

A project without a clearly defined audience rarely delivers lasting value.

---

### Building for everyone

Trying to satisfy every possible user often results in satisfying none.

Start with a clearly defined audience.

You can always expand later.
